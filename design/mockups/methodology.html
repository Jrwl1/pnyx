Methodology

How PNYX works

PNYX is a public record of political promises and party stances matched against available evidence. Our goal: make it easy for any Finnish citizen to see what their representatives and parties have committed to, and to check whether the record supports those commitments.

We track two kinds of political commitments: promises made by individual politicians, and stances taken by parties. These are related but separate — a politician's promise may align with, contradict, or be unrelated to their party's official position.

What we track

A "promise" is any public statement by a politician that makes a specific, verifiable claim about future action or policy intent. We draw these from Eduskunta transcripts, press conferences, campaign materials, and credible news coverage.

A "party stance" is an official party position on a policy area, drawn from party programmes, policy papers, or official party communications. Stances belong to parties, not to individual politicians.

Promise vs. stance

A promise is personal: "I will support increasing defence spending." A stance is institutional: "Our party supports increasing defence spending to 2.5% of GDP." PNYX tracks both and shows where they align or diverge.

Fulfillment status

Each promise has a fulfillment status that reflects whether the politician has delivered on their commitment. This is assessed based on available evidence and is separate from vote alignment.

Fulfilled
Available evidence indicates the politician has delivered on this promise. The commitment has been substantially met as stated.
Broken
Available evidence indicates the politician has acted contrary to this promise, or the commitment has been explicitly abandoned.
In progress
Evidence suggests the politician is actively working toward this commitment, but it has not yet been fully delivered.
Unknown
Fulfillment data is not yet available. This is not a negative judgment — it means PNYX has not yet assessed whether the commitment has been met. This is the default state for all promises until evidence is evaluated.

Vote alignment

Vote alignment tracks whether a politician's voting record in the Eduskunta supports or contradicts their promises. This requires linking roll-call vote data to specific promises.

Aligned
The politician's votes on related legislation are consistent with this promise.
Contradicted
The politician has voted against legislation that would support this promise, or in favor of legislation that contradicts it.
Mixed
The politician's voting record shows some votes supporting and some contradicting this promise.
Unknown
No voting record data has been linked to this promise. This is the default state until roll-call vote data is available.

Party context

Finland's multi-party parliamentary system means that a politician's commitments exist in the context of their party's positions. PNYX tracks this relationship explicitly.

Party stances are official party positions drawn from party programmes and policy papers. They are tracked separately from individual politician promises.

Party-line alignment shows where a politician's promises and votes align with or break from their party's official stances. A "party-line break" occurs when a politician's position materially contradicts their party's official stance on the same policy area.

Why party context matters

In Finnish politics, party discipline is significant but not absolute. Knowing when a politician follows or breaks from their party line helps voters understand who is an independent voice and who votes in lockstep. Both patterns are informative — neither is inherently good or bad.

Community sentiment

PNYX allows registered users to vote "support" or "oppose" on individual promises. This creates a community sentiment indicator.

Community sentiment is not a politician's voting record. It is not the party's position. It reflects how PNYX users feel about a promise. We display it transparently but label it clearly to prevent misinterpretation.

What we don't do

We do not rate or score politicians. There is no accountability score, letter grade, or ranking.

We do not editorialize. Our summaries describe what sources say, not what we think about them.

We do not infer fulfillment from incomplete data. If evidence is insufficient, we say "Unknown" — not "likely fulfilled" or "probably broken."

We do not infer vote alignment from community sentiment. User support/oppose votes are never used to assess a politician's actual voting record.

Handling uncertainty

One of PNYX's core principles: honest uncertainty is better than false confidence. When data is missing, we say so explicitly. Promise pages show "Unknown" with "Data not yet available." Politician profiles show how many promises lack fulfillment assessments.

Why "unknown" is not the same as "unfulfilled"

A promise with unknown fulfillment has not been assessed — it is not being judged negatively. Most promises will start as "unknown" and update as evidence is evaluated. This is a feature of transparency, not a bug.
